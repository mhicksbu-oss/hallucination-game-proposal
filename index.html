HALLUCINATION

What if the world changed whenever you stopped looking at it?

A developing game concept about unreliable perception. The player moves through a familiar place that repeatedly contradicts their memory, deciding which details to trust—and which version of reality to reinforce.

Reference game

The Ratchelor

Player experience

From 21 rats to one

Choose a Ratchelor, select seven contestants, meet them through short conversations, and give roses until only one potential soulmate remains.

Design observation

Rejection without cruelty

The elimination structure creates stakes, while epilogues give every rejected contestant a future. The competitive format is softened without removing choice.

03 / NARRATIVE + CONCEPT

Working hypothesis

A hallucination is a negotiation with missing evidence.

The game will not ask the player to identify one “correct” reality. It will ask how they behave when perception, memory, and external evidence disagree.

World

A familiar place with unstable continuity

Rooms, objects, text, and sounds change when attention moves elsewhere.

Player role

Observer and accidental author

Each act of noticing strengthens one version of the environment.

Intended experience

Curiosity → doubt → responsibility

The player gradually realizes that choosing what to trust also changes the world.

06 / TWO-WEEK BUILD PLAN

Production scope

Build the perceptual rule, then test whether players understand it.

W2

Build

One complete playable path

  • Opening and three short stages
  • Look-away / return state-change mechanic
  • Temporary visual and audio assets
  • One ending with tracked player choices
  • Basic accessibility and restart controls
W3

Test + refine

Make uncertainty legible

  • Observe at least three playtests
  • Test recognition of the core rule
  • Refine pacing, cues, and ending feedback
  • Replace temporary assets and complete credits
  • Document findings in this proposal
